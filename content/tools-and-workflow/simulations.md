# Simulations

Simulation models generate many possible slate outcomes from assumptions about player ranges and relationships. They can help compare lineups, but a model does not eliminate uncertainty or validate poor assumptions.

## Read distributions, not one score

Use simulation output to compare how often a lineup reaches a target, how often it falls short, and how its outcome changes with correlated players. A high average result may not be the best tournament construction if its top-end outcomes are heavily duplicated.

## Challenge the assumptions

Ask whether the player projections, correlations, and ownership inputs reflect current news. Test alternatives when a role is uncertain. The value of a simulation is the disciplined comparison it enables, not a guarantee of a winning lineup.
