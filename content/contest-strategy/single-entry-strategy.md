# Single-Entry Strategy

Single-entry contests limit every participant to one lineup. The restriction reduces the advantage of covering many lineup branches, so the goal is a focused roster with a clear slate read.

## Build around conviction

Begin with the game environment and player roles you trust most. A single-entry roster can include popular plays when their projection and opportunity support the decision. Differentiate through one or two lineup-level choices, such as a less common stack construction or a salary range the field may overlook.

## Avoid forced pivots

A low-owned player is not automatically a good single-entry play. Ask whether the player has a plausible ceiling, a usable role, and a connection to the lineup's game script. If not, the pivot only exchanges projection for uncertainty.

Before entry, write down the roster's path: which game produces the ceiling, which players benefit, and what makes the combination less common.
