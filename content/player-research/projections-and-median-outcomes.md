# Projections and Median Outcomes

A projection is a decision aid, not a promise. The median estimate describes a middle outcome across many possible games and gives a useful baseline for comparing salary and role.

## Use projections as a starting point

Compare a player's projection to nearby salaries and positions, then ask what assumptions support it. Expected volume, efficiency, playing time, and team scoring all contribute. A projection can become stale when injury news or a depth-chart change alters one of those assumptions.

## Do not stop at the median

Cash formats often place more weight on median outcomes. Tournaments need the upper portion of a player's range as well. Two players with a similar median may have different paths to a ceiling score because of target share, red-zone role, or game environment.

Document the reason for any major disagreement with a projection instead of changing it on instinct. Related: [[player-research/ceiling-and-floor|Ceiling and Floor]], [[player-research/usage-and-opportunity|Usage and Opportunity]], and [[player-research/index|Player Research]].
