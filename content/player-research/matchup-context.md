# Matchup Context

Matchup context adjusts a player read; it should not replace role analysis. Defensive tendencies, expected pace, travel, weather, and team style can matter when they change the volume or efficiency a player needs.

## Ask what changes

Instead of labeling a defense good or bad, identify the mechanism. Does the opponent encourage passes, limit rushing efficiency, create pressure, or concentrate targets toward a certain role? A useful matchup note explains how the player benefits or loses opportunity.

## Connect matchup to game script

A favored team may lean toward rushing late, while an underdog may need more passing volume. These are possible outcomes, not certainties. Use the spread and total as context, then compare the expected script with the player's salary and ceiling.
